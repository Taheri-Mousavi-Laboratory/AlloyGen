# Solidification interpretation Run evaluate_solidification on chosen Al–Ni–Er–Zr compositions, including Er/Zr refinements and matched Ni comparisons. Explain terminal ternary reservoir gain, primary L1₂ and competing Al₃Ni/Al₃Zr for the measured composition change. An Er/Zr change that helps equilibrium J or L12 may still worsen terminal phases or FR/CSC/HCS; assess that downstream cost on the exact composition. Identify diminishing returns and useful refinements without assuming monotonic HCS. Always label these as terminal Scheil amounts. Compare FR, CSC and HCS separately with the tool's reference; because HCS=CSC×FR/100, a lower CSC can coexist with higher HCS. Use returned CSC time intervals and FR start diagnostics when explaining this. Retrieve exact candidate results when a needed value is absent; request a scoped raw record for missing interval details. Do not infer printability or aged phase amounts from these proxies.

More terminal Scheil L1₂ does not establish more fine aged strengthening precipitates. A Ni-effect or plateau claim needs comparison at fixed Er/Zr, or explicit qualification of confounding composition changes.

When measured Ni addition gains reservoir but also substantial Al₃Ni, explain whether an intermediate Ni comparison could change the preferred compromise. If increasing Ni adds little reservoir while raising competing phases, consider whether a lower-Ni or intermediate comparison on the retained Er/Zr branch would change selection. State its decision value; do not require it after a matched comparison has already justified a defensible Ni choice. Compare reservoir retention, competing phases and FR/CSC/HCS; do not assume they improve together or invent a universal retention threshold. Identify the measured endpoints and unresolved tradeoff; choose the level from this evidence. Do not infer a plateau from two endpoints. Give Evidence → unresolved question → informative comparison → decision if favorable/unfavorable. In review, resolve your earlier suggestion as tested, still worth testing, or unnecessary with a scientific reason. Changing Ni requires exact final phase/J qualification.

Before supporting a final Ni choice, name the fixed Er/Zr branch of the comparison and check whether it matches the retained branch. If it does not, identify the missing matched comparison. A factual report can be accepted while this design work remains.

A large FR shared across Ni variants may originate upstream. Reading the saved scientific records identifies phases at the existing FR start cutoff. Use that evidence to ask whether an Er/Zr comparison could reduce the penalty while preserving J, service phase and feasibility; choose exact samples from this evidence.

When FR is the dominant remaining penalty, consult the saved scientific records for the candidate and reference before interpreting its cause. Report which phases are present at the cutoff and how the interval changes; distinguish this proxy behavior from a prediction of physical cracking probability.
