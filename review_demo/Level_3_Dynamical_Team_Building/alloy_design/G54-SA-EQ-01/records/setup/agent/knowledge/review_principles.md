# Reviewing scientific conclusions

Establish and retain a defensible qualified incumbent first. Use the task’s new Er/Zr refinement and matched-branch Ni comparison to resolve the current design tradeoff. State the determining evidence, remaining question and how either outcome could change selection. Existing measurements can answer a question; a valid unfavorable comparison can justify retaining the incumbent. Do not require another prescribed direction merely to complete a checklist. Physical explanation supports the design decision; it is not a separate open-ended investigation.

Among measured candidates satisfying the hard phase constraint, seek the lowest valid J. Use the task's absolute J tolerance against that lowest value to identify practical ties; then compare useful service-temperature L12 and phase/solidification costs within that band. A meaningful J disadvantage belongs in an explicitly labeled balanced alternative, not the coarsening-first preferred choice. Compare that alternative across the same indicators and state its J cost; do not introduce a hidden weighted score. Missing qualification of a promising lead calls for qualification, not silent rejection. An infeasible low-J point is not a feasible incumbent.

Before prioritizing secondary Ni tuning, ask what independent Er/Zr coarsening comparisons have actually been measured. A J tie between different Ni levels on one Er/Zr branch does not resolve the primary coarsening search. A failed coupled Er/Zr phase change does not show that an independent change is unpromising. If that primary-objective uncertainty could change the shortlist, obtain the coarsening/phase references and plan an informative independent contrast; otherwise give the evidence-based reason for deferring it. Do not equate completing a required comparison with resolving its broader design opportunity.

Check exact candidate/composition/source premises before consulting methods. Retrieved text does not confirm the question's premise. Available phase points, complete profiles and readiness for recommendation are distinct.

Separate correct scoped work from completion of the design mission. A required comparison conditional on the preferred choice becomes due when that choice is proposed; calling the choice "measured" or reporting a limitation does not waive it. A review may finish while identifying remaining required work; that is not approval to stop the design mission. Audit the few determining claims independently against exact records; a previous prose summary or selection rationale is not measured evidence. Use Claim | evidence | supported/correction needed, then state the conclusion and remaining design work.

Require a new Er/Zr composition investigation motivated by the initial assessment before declaring final design completion. Filling missing properties at an initial alloy or performing the initial Ni comparison does not replace that refinement. An unfavorable refinement can justify retaining the incumbent. Check whether the final Ni argument belongs to the retained Er/Zr branch. A resolved two-candidate comparison is not, by itself, a stopping argument for the original optimization task.

Feasible designs can still be improved: assess whether service-temperature phase gain, reduced competing phases or an unresolved solute interaction merits another comparison while respecting J. Zero high-temperature L12 is not an extra objective. Check the scientific rationale rather than demanding an arbitrary number of experiments.

A conditional experiment is a decision commitment, not a permanent search obligation. When its measured trigger is met, qualify the promising challenger through a documented calculation, or cite new evidence/a concrete limit that invalidates the trigger. Missing J/phase data cannot itself refute the value of measuring J/phase. For a cost-reduction experiment, retaining the desired benefit can satisfy its premise; do not change the criterion afterward to require a benefit increase. Keep the qualified incumbent while testing.

When multiple downstream comparisons share a large penalty, check whether you have considered its upstream origin using the relevant physical diagnostic. If a controlled midpoint retains favorable J and reduces competing-phase cost, check whether stopping leaves a consequential, testable interval unresolved. Resolve that question or state a concrete limit. Do not assume monotonicity or require every indicator to improve.
