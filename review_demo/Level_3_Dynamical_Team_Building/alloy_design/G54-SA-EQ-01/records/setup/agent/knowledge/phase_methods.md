# Phase-stability interpretation Interpret the equilibrium phase evidence relevant to the current task before recommending further measurements.

In planning, first make a compact matched-pair table. For each independently varied solute represented in the measured set, choose one informative pair, up to three rows: **case IDs | solute change and fixed other solutes | service L1₂ change | high-temperature L1₂ change and margin to the limit | competing-phase cost**. Check the compositions before calling a comparison controlled. If only coupled changes exist, label them as such; they do not identify an individual elemental effect. Report missing comparisons rather than inventing them. Use the existing table or the returned results or saved result files for the chosen IDs; do not repeat all results. Then give Evidence → unresolved question → informative comparison → decision if favorable/unfavorable. Choose the next compositions from the evidence. These local comparisons do not establish global monotonicity.

In execution, run evaluate_phase_stability on the chosen compositions. Compare equilibrium L1₂ at 250°C and 660°C against their separate purposes and the task's limit. Compare competing Al₃Zr amounts and upper disappearance behavior. Report liquid involvement and multiple disappearance intervals; a liquid-associated upper boundary is not a heat-treatment solvus. When a constraint is bracketed, identify the measured feasible/infeasible pair. Qualify the exact composition after other components change; do not apply results from a nearby alloy.

Use the returned results for values, sources and composition identity. A missing boundary does not invalidate known point phase amounts; preserve each qualification. Explain the phase conclusion and tradeoffs with the reference; cite saved J only as context, since phase evaluation does not calculate J. Report a zero phase amount as absent in that equilibrium; do not introduce an unprovided detection limit.

For a feasible low-J branch with substantial phase margin, identify a controlled perturbation that could improve service-temperature L1₂, using local evidence and phase expertise. Check both phase amounts and J; margin alone does not guarantee improvement. If a larger addition fails while a smaller one passes, recommend an intermediate comparison when useful rather than abandoning that direction. When more Zr improves J but raises Al3Zr, identify a possible intermediate comparison. A local Er penalty at one Zr does not resolve Er/Zr interaction. Zero L1₂ at 660°C is not an extra optimization target. In review, resolve your earlier suggestion as tested, still worth testing, or unnecessary with a scientific reason; qualification alone does not resolve it. Keep the J priority explicit and choose follow-up samples from this evidence.

Ask where Er/Zr is partitioning when useful L12 decreases: compare available L12 chemistry and competing Al3Zr amounts at the same temperature. Separate amount from chemistry and equilibrium from Scheil evidence. This can explain why lower J and higher competing-phase cost coexist; it is a question to test, not a guaranteed elemental trend. Report the high-temperature L12 constraint separately as pass/fail; among passing alloys, a larger or smaller value is neither a favorable nor an unfavorable design indicator.
